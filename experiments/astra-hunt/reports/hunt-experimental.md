### EXPERIMENTAL-1: Zero contour reach emits degenerate faces with zero normals
- Severity: P1 (wrong geometry on a documented helper combination used by shipped samples)
- Repro: `scratch/hunt-experimental/hunt-experimental-contour-zero.ts` — run `npx tsx scratch/hunt-experimental/hunt-experimental-contour-zero.ts`
- Expected: `src/experimental/contour.ts` says a section is a closed cross-section and the contour helper promises sweep behavior; `src/experimental/contour.ts:42-44` only advises keeping reaches above zero except a point cap. A flattened endpoint should either be represented with valid cap/degenerate handling or be rejected clearly; it must not silently produce invalid normals. This exact pattern is used by `samples/lifeguardContour.ts:251-271` and `samples/sprinterContour.ts` for hair (`front` starts at `0`, flat caps).
- Actual: The minimal path with `front: 0, back: 0.05, side: 0.05, sides: 12` and `caps: { start: "flat", end: "flat" }` builds 50 vertices/48 triangles, of which 8 vertices have the exact normal `[0, 0, 0]`; 10 of the 48 triangles have zero area. The two shipped contour samples reproduce it: each hair mesh has 6 zero-normal vertices (sample audit `scratch/hunt-experimental/hunt-experimental-contour-normals.ts`). The collapsed front half of each ring creates duplicate/degenerate faces; smooth normal generation leaves those vertices unshaded/invalid.
- Cause: `src/experimental/contour.ts:181-211` clamps reaches to zero but still emits all polygon vertices; `src/sweep.ts:753-785` and flat-cap generation connect those duplicate points without collapsing them or rejecting the zero reach. `BufferGeometry.computeVertexNormals()` consequently leaves zero normals.
- Fix: Reject zero reaches except for a true point cap with a named error, or collapse duplicate contour vertices/skip zero-area cap triangles and assign a valid endpoint normal when a flattened section is intentionally supported.
