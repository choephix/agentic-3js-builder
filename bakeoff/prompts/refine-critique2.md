Look at the model as the art director who wrote the brief, and decide what stands between it and an astonishing result for this subject and style. Judge in this order and spend the turn where the model gains most:

1. **Identity and form.** Does the subject read instantly as itself, with the anatomy or structure, proportions and silhouette its style calls for? Is the face appealing and clear in the close-up: eyes visible and well placed, features in proportion, nothing crowding or hiding them?
2. **Soundness.** Every part seated where it belongs: touching what holds it, not sunk, not clipping through its neighbours, facing the right way, thin planes turned so they read from the main views. Check every view, the back included.
3. **Composition.** The subject fills most of the frame and nothing in the setting hides it; the base frames it closely.
4. **Finish.** Textures, colour, glow and small details on the subject itself.

A structural fix (rebuilding a muzzle, re-angling wings, reshaping a body, reseating a part) is worth more than any added ornament, so make those first and make them fully. Atmosphere and loose particles stay as they are unless they hurt the read.
